# Ghonchu: the concept

## Who Ghonchu is

A round, glowing, slightly goofy tutor with big eyes. Ghonchu does not do the work for a learner: it looks through the phone's camera, asks, nudges and speaks aloud through the browser, so a child building a robot hears "check the ground wire first" instead of reading a manual. On the site it bounces, hops and follows your finger; in the notebooks it is the voice of every step.

## How Ghonchu fits the course

- **The robots.** The simplest possible plastic two-wheel and four-wheel cars first, then drones, boats and robot arms, to try different forms of embodiment. Arduino first, then ESP32 and higher-end boards. Parts cost as little as possible (the target in India is under ₹1,000) so a school anywhere can start.
- **Simulation and the real robot.** What a learner does in simulation is meant to carry over to the real robot with little difference.
- **Connected robots.** Every robot is IoT, drivable from anywhere with an internet connection, including by brain-computer interface through a NeuroSky MindWave headset (notebooks 02, 09, 14 and 16).
- **Their own language.** Children code their robots in their own language through the Hindawi Programming System; the Shaili Robot's first moves are upar, neeche, bayein and dayein, and Ghonchu says them on the site.
- **A phone is enough.** Colab notebooks run in a phone's browser, and the camera Ghonchu looks through is the phone's own.
- **The first lesson in three timed parts.** Ten minutes to build (notebooks 00 and 01), thirty to connect (02 and 16), fifteen to tune, ending with a safety heartbeat that stops the motors when messages stop arriving.
- **Upwards from there.** The same kind of notebook serves GATE-level courses in computer science and in robotics and automation, and research: assistants for clinics, field archaeology, linguistics, psychology and BCI (05 to 10), systems architecture in SysML for a CubeSat (11), apps and full stacks from a sentence (12 and 13), notebooks joined into a volunteer grid (14), and the operating-systems course in which every atomic function is specified first and filled afterwards (15).

## Discussed, not yet built

Flashing a robot over the air from a binary served by GitHub Pages, over Web Serial through a phone's OTG cable, or in the Wokwi simulator for learners without hardware; the shared sheet and form behind the backbone; Ghonchu's own build checks beyond the light level; and the Hindawi environment cell's release address.

## Privacy, safety and use worldwide

Ghonchu is meant for children and researchers in every country. The camera notebooks open with a privacy note, the stricter rule applies wherever two frameworks differ, and nothing here is a medical, psychological or diagnostic device.
